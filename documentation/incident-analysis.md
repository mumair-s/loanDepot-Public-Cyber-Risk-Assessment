# Incident Analysis

## What the Public Record Establishes

The January 2024 public disclosures establish several important facts:

- an unauthorized third party accessed certain loanDepot systems;
- data was encrypted;
- certain systems were shut down during response;
- loan-origination, loan-servicing, and customer portals required restoration;
- sensitive personal information was accessed;
- the affected population was reported at approximately 16.9 million people;
- consumer notices identified highly sensitive data categories;
- the company incurred material incident-related expenses;
- lawsuits and regulatory engagement followed.

These facts support confidentiality, availability, financial, legal/regulatory, and reputational risk themes.

## What the Public Record Does Not Establish

The sources used in this project do not establish:

- the initial access vector,
- whether credentials were stolen,
- whether MFA was bypassed or absent,
- whether a known vulnerability was exploited,
- the attacker's identity,
- whether a third party was the attack path,
- exact dwell time beyond the publicly reported access window,
- the internal effectiveness of specific security controls.

That means a defensible analysis must not convert those unknowns into facts.

## CIA Triad Connection

### Confidentiality

Sensitive personal information was accessed. The consumer notice lists personal and financial data, supporting a severe confidentiality impact.

### Integrity

The initial SEC filing says data was encrypted by the unauthorized third party. Public information does not establish broader unauthorized modification of business records, so the project does not make that claim.

### Availability

Certain systems were shut down, and core origination/servicing/customer-portal systems later required restoration. This demonstrates availability and business-resilience impact.

## Business Impact

Cyber events are not only technical problems.

The public record demonstrates impact in several business areas:

- customer notification and identity-protection obligations,
- operational recovery,
- legal/litigation exposure,
- regulatory engagement,
- direct financial expense,
- insurance recovery,
- management and governance involvement.

This is why the project converts incident facts into business-risk statements rather than stopping at technical observations.
